Getting a driving licence in Barcelona

For a resident with no previous licence, holding the residence card of a family member of an EU citizen. Rules, prices and contacts link to their sources; points that still need confirming are marked to confirm.

At a glance

What has to be done
The full Spanish B licence: a medical report, a theory exam and a practical exam. With no licence from any country, there is nothing to exchange (a Russian licence could not have been exchanged anyway: Russia has no exchange agreement with Spain).[1]
Expected cost
About 620 € to 1,390 € with the DGT fee, medical report not included, mainly depending on the number of driving lessons (5 to 20 in the examples below).
Funding
No public or private scheme paying for a B licence in Barcelona was found for 2026. Two free registrations open the door to employment programmes and are worth doing anyway.
Languages
The theory exam can be taken in Spanish, Catalan, English, French or German. Russian is not on the DGT's list.

The procedure, step by step

  1. Medical and psychotechnical report

    Issued by an approved medical centre for drivers (centro de reconocimiento de conductores). The report is valid for 90 days: the exam application has to be filed before it expires.[3]

    The price is set by each centre. to confirm Ask for a quote when booking.

  2. Choose a driving school (autoescuela)

    As a general rule, Spanish regulations require training in an authorised driving school before the practical exam on open roads; a candidate without that training is not admitted to it.[5]

    The theory can be studied alone and the exam application can be filed directly with the DGT (Dirección General de Tráfico, the Spanish traffic authority),[2] but in practice a school is needed for the driving lessons.[5]

  3. Open the file with the DGT and pay the fee

    The file contains:[2]

    • the official application form (Mod. 04);
    • the medical report from step 1;
    • proof of payment of the DGT fee 2.1: 94.05 €, which covers two exam attempts. After two failures, a new application and a new fee are needed;
    • for a non-EU national: a valid residence authorisation.

    A foreign applicant must also show normal residence in Spain of at least six months.[5] The DGT defines normal residence as at least 185 days per calendar year, in a regular situation.[4]

    to confirmThe residence card of a family member of an EU citizen is not named as such on the DGT list. Its acceptance has to be confirmed with the driving school or the Barcelona traffic office (Jefatura de Tráfico).

    The DGT fee is paid separately from the driving school's prices.[8][9]

  4. Theory exam
    • 30 questions, three possible answers each; pass with no more than 3 errors (10 % of the questions).[5][7]
    • Languages: Spanish, the co-official languages (Catalan in Barcelona), English, French and German. An easy-read version exists. Russian is not on the list.[6]
    • The DGT has announced questions based on short videos to test risk perception; the exam time is extended accordingly.[7]
    • Free official practice tests, without registration, to get familiar with the format: DGT practice tests. There are only two or three questionnaires per licence type, so the DGT itself says they are not a study tool.[3]

    A passed theory exam stays valid for 2 years. If the licence is not obtained within that period, all tests must be taken again.[3]

  5. Driving lessons and practical exam

    Lessons and the exam take place in the school's dual-control car, with the instructor.[5] The number of lessons depends on the learner and is the main cost variable.

    Waiting times for the practical exam are a known issue in Catalonia. In April 2026, Catalan driving schools reported more than 85,000 people waiting.[10] In June 2026 the DGT disputed the schools' waiting-list figures in general, saying they count everyone who has passed the theory and still has two years to take the practical exam.[11] Starting early helps.

What it costs: two Barcelona schools

Prices published by the schools themselves (Alba states that VAT is included; Corsa does not say). The DGT fee (94.05 €) and the medical report come on top.

Worked examples, medical report not included:

  • Alba Pack A (5 lessons) plus the DGT fee: 522 + 94.05 = about 616 €.
  • Alba Pack B (10 lessons) plus the DGT fee: 710 + 94.05 = about 804 €.
  • Corsa with 20 lessons: theory course 279 € + theory exam 34.95 € + 20 lessons 880 € + practical exam 99 € = about 1,293 €; with the DGT fee, about 1,387 €.

Fewer lessons cost less; more lessons or a failed exam raise the bill.

Corsa also advertises courses and material in English.[9]

Ways to spend less. Compare several schools, check what each pack includes, ask about payment in instalments, and book the lessons once the theory is passed.

Funding: what exists, what does not

No scheme paying for a B licence in Barcelona in 2026 was found, after checking the Catalan employment service (SOC), the youth guarantee and the Generalitat's grants for unemployed people in training, none of which mention a B licence.[12][14] The private programme #EllasConducen (Midas with Ayuda en Acción) is described as helping women taking part in Ayuda en Acción programmes (five in Alicante in 2021, about ten by 2023); no open call for the public was found.[15]

Two free registrations

confirmedRegister as a jobseeker with the SOC (Servei d'Ocupació de Catalunya).[13] The online procedure requires a Spanish DNI; people with a NIE linked to a residence authorisation can request registration by phone:

930 886 200

Monday to Friday, 8:30 to 14:00. Ask during the call what follows; for online registrations, the SOC requires contacting the local employment office (oficina de treball) within 30 days.

confirmedRegister with the youth guarantee (Garantía Juvenil), for people over 16 and under 30 who were neither working nor in education or training the previous day, registered as residents (empadronados) in Catalonia, with a residence authorisation that allows work.[14] Registering as a jobseeker also registers you with it, if you meet these conditions.[14]

Online (electronic ID, digital certificate, Cl@ve or a username and password): garantiajuvenil.sepe.es. Also possible in person at the employment office.

It gives access to training, internships and job offers; no licence funding is listed.

Worth asking directly

  • The employment office, once registered: whether any 2026 programme includes the licence, for instance one linked to a specific job.
  • The district social services and Barcelona Activa, the city's employment agency (its website was under maintenance on 16 October 2026).
  • Charities working with migrant women in Barcelona (Red Cross, Càritas): no licence support was found online, which does not rule it out.
